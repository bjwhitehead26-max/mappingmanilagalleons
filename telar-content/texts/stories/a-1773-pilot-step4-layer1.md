The normal route for Manila-Acapulco galleons from the late-sixteenth century up to the mid-eighteenth century was that they departed Manila bay, sailed between Mindoro and Luzon, through the straights in the center of the Philippine islands, around Ticao, and then northeast out through the San Bernardino Straight. From that point, the vessels would sail northeast to about 36 degrees of latitude to catch the easterly trade winds. Once they reached the Californian coast, they made their ways south to Acapulco.
However, the Consolación's 1773 voyage took the vessel past Mindoro, and then straight south to Mindanao. After exploring a series of islands, the frigate then made its way to the open Pacific.
