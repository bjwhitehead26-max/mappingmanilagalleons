There are several important questions about this source that remain unanswered due to what remains of the document. First, the pilots are unnamed. This is very atypical for this type of document. As such, we don't know who is writing. However, by closely analyzing the style of writing, it becomes aparent that this documnt was written by at least two different people. If you look at the image of page 4, beginning with the dated entry on  **January 27**, the handwriting becomes obviously clearer and more refined. There are multiple things this could signify: The head pilot (piloto mayor) either left the vessel, became ill, or died. As such, the second pilot (piloto segundo) took charge of tracking the ship's progress. Furthermore, this type of source was always written by highly educated men (often of European decent) trained in navigation and cosmology. As one would expect, there are usually very little mentions of the lower sailors of the ships. However, by closely reading certain sections, like in the previous page where I noted the brief mention of the crew being highly diverse containing several different Philippine ethnic/linguistic groups, we can at least understand that there was always a melting pot of culture on the decks of the galleons. Even if not explicitly mentioned, the pilots relied on local expertise for both knowledge and labor to navigate the Philippines and cross the Pacific.
