On Febuary 14, the frigate spotted a potential Dutch settlement on a small island in the southern Philippines around 9 degrees of Latitude which they mentioned for probable future investigation.
By April 6, the frigate had sailed south through the Philippines towards the large island of Mindanao. Near there, they encountered a series of islands around 5 degrees that the pilot explained were not on their maps. They note that the uncharted island homed an indigenous group with thatched huts constructed beneath coconut palms. The group shouted and waived their spears towards the vessel to prevent them from landing. The pilot made note that the frigate's crew consisted of sailors from all across the Philippines, yet none of them could comprehend the words of this indigenous community before them.
As the vessel made the trans-Pacific crossing, they encountered a vicous thirty-hour-long storm on April 15 and 16. By March 1, the frigate's sails and rigging were torn to shreds and had to be replaced. Though the relations of the actual trans-Pacific crossing was brief, this detail alone reveals that the journey was rough.
